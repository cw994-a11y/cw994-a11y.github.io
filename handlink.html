Research questionHow far can hand motion stand in for object tracking?

01 / The opportunity

Make the prop
simpler.

Matching 3D-printed objects supply real contact surfaces. The headset supplies hand poses. The proposed system estimates object movement through hand–object constraints.

“Tracker-free” refers to the physical prop. Headset cameras and hand tracking remain essential.

Design choiceHandLink approach

Where motion comes from

Object-mounted trackers measure a prop’s pose. HandLink proposes estimating it from the tracked hand during constrained contact.

When the real world is visible

MR passthrough supports manual alignment; interaction then takes place in immersive VR.

What the user holds

A matching printed prop with no embedded electronics. The aim is easier fabrication and less per-object setup.

Building on prior passive-haptics research, this project studies hand-referenced motion estimation for uninstrumented props.

02 / Proposed mechanism

Align once.
Estimate through contact.

Initial registration is a starting point. Movement after calibration must be inferred and checked.

01

Match & register

Print the virtual model at matching dimensions. In passthrough, manually overlap the two objects, then switch to VR.

02

Infer a stable grasp

Use palm and finger contacts to identify candidate grasps. Opposing contacts and tracking confidence would gate the estimate.

03

Follow the hand

During a stable, non-slipping grasp, retain the hand–object offset and update the virtual target from the hand pose.

04

Simulate & recalibrate

Use rigid-body dynamics for contact and release. Tracking loss, slip or drift calls for re-alignment.

Core pose estimate / Proposed

C = TWH(tg)−1 TWO(tg)
T̂WO(t) = TWH(t) C

A stable grasp preserves the object’s relative pose in the hand. Moving the hand updates the object’s predicted position and orientation.

TWH
Tracked hand pose in world coordinates
TWO
Virtual object pose in world coordinates
tg, C
Grasp onset and fixed hand–object offset

Assumes rigid, no-slip contact and a consistent tracking frame. This predicts a pose; it does not measure the physical object.

Technical detail / Contact, dynamics & error measurement +

Proposed mathematical formulation developed from the concept. These equations are a development plan, not verified implementation results.

01 / Candidate contact

ci = 1[ |φO(xi)| < ε ]

xi is a tracked finger or palm sample; φO is its signed distance to the virtual object surface. ε is a tunable tolerance. Contacts from multiple fingers and opposing surface normals can nominate a grasp; proximity alone does not establish a stable physical grasp.

02 / Compliant virtual motion

F = kp(p* − p) + kd(v* − v)
τ = kR Log(R*Rᵀ)∨ + kω(ω* − ω)

Position p* and rotation R* come from the target pose; v* and ω* are target velocities. Gains tune a spring–damper response. Log(·)∨ gives the rotation-error vector. Unity can apply force and torque, or implement a corresponding joint drive.

03 / Release & surface contact

m v̇ = mg + Σfi
‖ft‖ ≤ μ fn

After release, gravity and contact forces govern virtual motion. μ bounds simulated tangential friction relative to normal force. These parameters describe the virtual model; physical weight, friction and slip remain unmeasured.

04 / Test the estimate

ep = ‖p̂ − pGT‖
eR = acos(clamp((tr(R̂ᵀRGT) − 1)/2, −1, 1))

Compare estimated and independently measured ground-truth poses. Position error and angular error reveal drift. Evaluation-only tracking can provide ground truth without becoming a requirement of the proposed deployed system.

03 / Prototype → research

What works.
What comes next.

Implemented / Initial demo

A physical hand model.

Palm and finger-joint colliders push virtual Rigidbody objects. One-button MR/VR switching supports the alignment workflow. The supplied recording shows hand-driven virtual interaction.

Proposed / Next phase

Reliable object estimation.

Develop multi-finger grasp recognition, lifting, rotation and release. Compare prop fidelity using task time, accuracy, perceived control and physical–virtual alignment error.

04 / Future applications

Small props.
More places to practice.

Directions to investigate after validating the interaction method.

01 / Rehabilitation

Everyday hand tasks.

Clinician-guided home practice in virtual kitchens, restaurants or shops. Accessibility and therapeutic benefit would require clinical evaluation.

02 / Collaboration

A shared thing to hold.

Remote designers could handle matching local props and share virtual changes. Physical forces would not be transmitted between sites.

03 / Training

Tools in context.

Printed tool replicas could support procedural practice in simulated workspaces, with physical feedback limited to the actual prop.
