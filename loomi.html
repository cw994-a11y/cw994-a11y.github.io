01 / Human-centered design

Research shaped
every iteration.

From interaction alternatives to a tested prototype: design decisions grounded in exploration, user feedback and repeated making.

01

Explore before choosing

Mindmapping and a cultural probe framed moments of connection. A morphological chart compared inputs, outputs, form, materials and color.

Design decision LED + OLED outputs, with physical button input.

02

Build to learn

Each prototype expanded what could be tested: from basic light to messages and a refined physical form.

03

Evaluate with people

Five men, ages 18–21, participated in formative UX surveys, SUS and think-aloud testing.

81.5/100Reported SUS
4.4/5Would use again
04

Translate feedback into changes

Each finding informed a specific change in function or form.

Think-aloud / Words
“It is nice if OLED displayed text ‘thinking of you’.”

Response Added text functions; refined text size and alignment.

Think-aloud / Input
“The input system is too simple; I want to see something other than button.”

Response Explored rotary input for finer physical control.

From feedback to form

“I want a device that is portable and has a minimalist style.”
UX survey feedback · Smaller, smoother enclosure
Research scope & next steps

The early cultural probe involved five participants over 48 hours. Evaluation scores are source-reported, not independently recalculated. No post-redesign retest or longitudinal benefit is established. Next: evaluate the revised object with more diverse pairs in everyday use.
